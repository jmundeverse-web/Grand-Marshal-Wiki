THE FOUNDATION OF EXISTENCE

The Six Laws

The Six Laws are fundamental principles of existence. They are distinct from divine concepts and Authorities. A concept such as War or Death may be possessed, embodied, or exercised by divine beings without necessarily being one of the Six Laws.

LAW I

Creation

THE ORIGIN

The Law of Creation concerns the emergence of existence itself. It governs the fundamental transition by which something comes into being rather than remaining absent.

The principle of beginning.
LAW II

Causality

THE WEAVER

The Law of Causality governs the relationship between events, actions, conditions, and consequences. It establishes that occurrences do not exist independently from the chains that connect them.

Every effect belongs to a chain of cause.
LAW III

Ending

THE LAST WITNESS

The Law of Ending governs conclusion, departure, and the eventual cessation of things that have begun. It represents the principle that existence does not remain indefinitely unchanged.

All that begins possesses an ending.
LAW IV

Actuality

THE IMMUTABLE

The Law of Actuality concerns what truly is. It distinguishes existence and actuality from possibility, illusion, interpretation, or unrealized potential.

What is, is.
LAW V

Convergence

THE CONVERGENT

The Law of Convergence governs the meeting and intersection of separate paths. Different causes, beings, events, and histories may move independently before eventually reaching a common point.

Separate paths may converge.
LAW VI

Conception

THE UNFORMED

The Law of Conception concerns the formation of possibilities before they become fully established within actuality. Its deeper nature remains part of the developing cosmological archive.

Possibility precedes manifestation.

The Law Embodiment Principle

A Law is conserved through its embodiment. A Law that already possesses an active embodiment cannot simply be embodied by another being at the same time. When its bearer dies, the Law becomes vacant. Even beings capable of reaching the highest known levels of divine development cannot simply embody an occupied Law.

Laws and Divine Authorities

The Six Laws should not be confused with divine concepts or Authorities. War, Death, Dreams, and similar concepts can exist as divine powers without replacing the Six Laws. The distinction between a Law, a concept, an Authority, and a divine domain is fundamental to the cosmology.
